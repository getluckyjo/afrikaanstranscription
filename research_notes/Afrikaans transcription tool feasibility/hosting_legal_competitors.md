# Hosting economics, legal/privacy, competitors and business models for a FREE Afrikaans speech-to-text service (state as of 19 Oct 2026)

Scope note: prices are USD unless marked; EUR/ZAR where the source quotes them. "Flagged" = uncertain, stale, or derived by me rather than quoted. All derived per-hour-of-audio numbers are my arithmetic from the cited unit prices.

## Key Question 1: What does transcription cost per hour of audio on managed APIs / serverless GPU?

### Takeaway
Managed Whisper APIs now cost $0.01–$0.04 per hour of audio (Deepinfra, Cloudflare, Groq, fal), i.e. 10–30x cheaper than OpenAI's $0.18–$0.36/hr, and Cloudflare's free allocation (10,000 neurons/day) alone covers roughly 3.5 hours of audio per day at zero cost. Serverless GPUs (Modal/RunPod) only beat these per-minute APIs if you keep a GPU busy with batched large-v3-turbo.

### Inferences
- Implied cost per hour of audio, managed APIs: Deepinfra ≈ $0.012/hr; Cloudflare ≈ $0.03/hr ($0.0005×60; $0.0308 using the 46.63-neuron figure); Groq turbo $0.04/hr; fal ≈ $0.03/hr (stale); OpenAI $0.18–$0.36/hr; Deepgram $0.26–$0.31/hr; ElevenLabs $0.40/hr.
- Cloudflare's free 10,000 neurons/day ÷ 46.63 neurons/min ≈ 214 audio minutes/day ≈ 3.6 h/day ≈ 107 h/month at $0 (and no card beyond the free Workers plan). Groq's 28,800 audio-seconds/day = 8 h of audio/day ≈ 240 h/month at $0 if those limits apply to the free tier.
- Serverless GPU only wins at scale: on a RunPod serverless 4090 at $1.10/hr, an effective batched throughput of ~150x realtime (see KQ2) gives ≈ $0.007 per audio hour; on Modal L4 at $0.80/hr with ~60x effective → ≈ $0.013/hr. Cold starts, model loading and sub-minute voice notes push the effective number up, so for a WhatsApp-voice-note workload (many 10–60 s clips) a per-minute API is cheaper and simpler.

## Key Question 2: Dedicated GPU rental, realistic throughput, and CPU-only options

### Takeaway
A rented RTX 4090 costs $0.13–$0.89/hr depending on marketplace tier and large-v3-turbo transcribes hundreds of hours of audio per GPU-hour when batched, so a single busy consumer GPU costs well under $0.01 per audio hour; CPU-only on a €16/month VPS is viable for low volume but no clean benchmark for turbo int8 on a Hetzner CX exists.

### Cited Findings

- RunPod: RTX 4090 "from $0.34/hr" (Community Cloud) and $0.69/hr Secure Cloud; A100 SXM 80GB Community $1.39/hr; A40 $0.44/hr; spot 4090 $0.32–$0.44/hr — [Tech Insider comparison, 2026](https://tech-insider.org/runpod-vs-lambda-vs-vast-ai-2026/) (flagged: aggregator). RunPod's own page: RTX 4090 pods $0.89/hr (visible table) with "from $0.34/hr" on the GPU page; L4 $0.59/hr; A40 $0.59/hr; A100 PCIe/SXM $1.79/hr — [RunPod 4090 page](https://www.runpod.io/gpu-models/rtx-4090), [RunPod pricing](https://www.runpod.io/pricing).
- Vast.ai: RTX 4090 listed floor $0.13/hr, median $0.49/hr; RTX 3090 floor $0.08/hr, median $0.26/hr; RTX 5090 floor $0.37, median $0.67 (live rates, update hourly) — [Vast.ai pricing](https://vast.ai/pricing), [Vast.ai 4090 page: "for $0.16/hr"](https://vast.ai/pricing/gpu/RTX-4090). Throughput (Whisper large-v3-turbo):
- TheStage AI benchmark of the original `whisper-large-v3-turbo` on an RTX 4090: RTFx 164 at batch size 1, 779 at batch size 24 (their optimised variants reach 254–270 and 954–1,014) — [HF: TheStageAI/Elastic-whisper-large-v3-turbo](https://huggingface.co/TheStageAI/Elastic-whisper-large-v3-turbo).
- faster-whisper maintainers' GPU benchmark: large-v3 fp16 beam 5 = 52.0 s, 4,521 MB VRAM; large-v3-turbo fp16 = 19.2 s, 2,537 MB; turbo int8 = 19.6 s, 1,545 MB — [faster-whisper issue #1030](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper/issues/1030). A community table in the same thread: large-v3-turbo 13.40% WER at 129.5x realtime vs large-v3 13.20% at 55.3x — [same issue](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper/issues/1030).
- Salad benchmark (large-v3, consumer GPUs): "1 Million hours of audio transcription for just $5,110", RTX 4080 best for long files with an average real-time factor of 40 — [Salad blog](https://blog.salad.com/whisper-large-v3) (= $0.0051 per audio hour, my arithmetic).
- Whisper large-v3-turbo on an H100 reached 597x realtime in one vendor benchmark — [inferencebench.io](https://inferencebench.io/blog/whisper-large-v3-turbo-597x-realtime-asr-benchmark/) (flagged: vendor).
- Turbo is 809M parameters, ~6 GB VRAM fp16, 1.6 GiB GGML on disk, ~2.3 GB RAM in whisper.cpp, "~8x" the speed of large-v3 — [OpenWhispr model-size table](https://openwhispr.com/blog/whisper-model-sizes-explained) (flagged: secondary).

CPU-only:
- Community benchmarks with faster-whisper: large-v3-turbo 19.155 s fp16 / 19.591 s int8 vs 143 s for full large-v3 on the same file — [Tessera summary](https://tesseraai.cloud/en/blog/whisper-large-v3-turbo-vs-large-v3-cpu-eu/), and "CPU (faster-whisper, i7): ~143 s per file large-v3 vs ~20 s turbo (~6x)" — [Vexascribe](https://vexascribe.com/whisper-large-v3-vs-turbo) (flagged: both are secondary summaries of the faster-whisper README benchmark; clip length not stated in the snippet).
- PromptQuorum 2026 head-to-head: CPU-only x86 `base` model ~15x (whisper.cpp) / ~20x (faster-whisper) realtime; large-v3 on RTX 4070 ~8x (whisper.cpp) / ~12x (faster-whisper); Apple M-series Metal ~10x for large-v3 — [PromptQuorum](https://www.promptquorum.com/power-local-llm/local-whisper-stt-comparison-2026) (flagged: unbatched figures).
- Hetzner Cloud after the June 2026 price increase (Germany/Finland): CX23 2 vCPU/4 GB €5.49/mo, CX33 4 vCPU/8 GB €8.49, CX43 8 vCPU/16 GB €15.99, CX53 16 vCPU/32 GB €29.49; dedicated-vCPU CCX13 €42.99, CCX33 8 vCPU €138.49 — [CostGoat Hetzner calculator, Oct 2026](https://costgoat.com/pricing/hetzner), [Northflank on the 2026 increases](https://northflank.com/blog/hetzner-cloud-server-price-increases). Shared CX plans can "only be bought when Hetzner has capacity" — [Better Stack review](https://betterstack.com/community/guides/web-servers/hetzner-cloud-review/).

### Inferences
- Hours of audio per GPU-hour: a 4090 running batched turbo at the measured RTFx 779 would do ~779 audio-hours per GPU-hour; allowing for VAD, I/O, short clips and queueing, 100–300x is a realistic planning figure. At Vast median $0.49/hr that is $0.0016–$0.005 per audio hour; at RunPod Secure $0.89/hr, $0.003–$0.009.
- A dedicated GPU is a fixed cost ($250–$650/month for a 4090 24/7; ~€250/month Hetzner GEX45) that only makes sense above roughly 5,000–10,000 audio hours/month; below that, per-minute APIs or serverless are cheaper.
- CPU-only: with turbo int8 on an 8-vCPU CX43 (€15.99) I would expect ~1–3x realtime (no direct source), i.e. ~700–2,000 audio hours/month if saturated, giving ~€0.01–€0.02 per audio hour but with multi-minute latency per file. Flagged estimate.

### Gaps
- No published benchmark of faster-whisper large-v3-turbo int8 on Hetzner CX/CPX vCPUs; the CPU figures above are from laptop/desktop i7 chips.
- Hetzner GEX45 monthly price cap could not be read cleanly from the page.
- A10 and L4 dedicated hourly rates on RunPod/Vast were not captured beyond RunPod L4 $0.59/hr.

## Key Question 3: Browser / on-device options and whether platform speech APIs support Afrikaans

### Takeaway
Neither Apple's dictation/SFSpeechRecognizer locales nor Google's on-device recognisers (Pixel Recorder, ML Kit GenAI Speech Recognition) list Afrikaans, so a "zero-cost backend" via native OS speech APIs is not available for Afrikaans; the only genuinely free client-side route is Whisper itself running in the browser via WebGPU/WASM (transformers.js / whisper.cpp), which works but needs a capable device and a ~1.6 GB model download.

### Cited Findings
- Apple iOS/iPadOS 27 feature availability: the "Dictation" and "Dictation: On-Device and Modeless Dictation" language lists include English (South Africa) but not Afrikaans; Live Captions and Notes transcription lists also omit Afrikaans — [Apple feature availability](https://www.apple.com/ios/feature-availability/) (flagged: page is 180k characters; the first 100k were read and the lists appeared complete). Apple's SpeechAnalyzer "supports the same locales that are supported by the keyboard's dictation feature" — [objc2 docs mirroring Apple](https://docs.rs/objc2-speech/latest/objc2_speech/struct.SFSpeechRecognizer.html), [WWDC25 SpeechAnalyzer](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/277/).
- Google Pixel Recorder: real-time transcription languages (Pixel 6+) are Mandarin, English variants, French, German, Hindi, Italian, Japanese, Spanish; "Re-transcribe" adds many languages including isiXhosa, Setswana, Southern Sotho, Swati, Tsonga and Venda (South Africa) but not Afrikaans; re-transcribe "may process audio files on Google servers" — [Pixel Phone Help](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/16267698?hl=en).
- Google ML Kit GenAI on-device Speech Recognition: Basic mode (Android API 31+) supports en-US plus fr, it, de, es, hi, ja, pt-BR, tr, pl, zh, ko, ru, vi (beta); Advanced mode (Pixel 10/11) adds nl, da, sv, id, ar, th; Afrikaans is not listed — [ML Kit docs](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/genai/speech-recognition/android).
- Android `SpeechRecognizer`: "The set of supported languages for speech recognition can vary based on the device and OS version" — [Medium guide quoting the API](https://medium.com/@andraz.pajtler/android-speech-to-text-the-missing-guide-part-1-824e2636c45a); official reference — [developer.android.com](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/speech/SpeechRecognizer).
- Google's cloud voice typing (Docs) supports Afrikaans — [Google Docs voice typing languages](https://support.google.com/docs/answer/4492226?hl=en). Google Cloud STT v2 chirp models support af-ZA — [Google Cloud STT](https://docs.cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/docs/speech-to-text-supported-languages).
- Live Transcribe: "real-time in over 80 languages and dialects" — [Google Play listing](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.audio.hearing.visualization.accessibility.scribe); offline downloads are available on Pixel/select devices (Android 12+) for "English, French and more" but the help page prints no language list — [Android Accessibility Help](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/9158064?hl=en).
- Google Meet live captions list "Afrikaans (beta)" — [Google Meet help](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/15077804?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop); translated captions support 69 languages including Afrikaans — [Workspace Updates, June 2024](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2024/06/more-languages-for-captions-and-translated-captions-in-google-meet.html).
- Microsoft Teams meeting live captions: Afrikaans is not in the spoken-language list (nor in the meeting translation list); Afrikaans appears only as a translation target for Teams live events — [Teams meetings captions](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/teams/meetings/use-live-captions-in-microsoft-teams-meetings), [Teams live events](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/teams/meetings/use-live-captions-in-a-live-event-in-microsoft-teams).
- Browser Whisper: a public WebGPU demo runs `whisper-large-v3-turbo` entirely in-browser with transformers.js — [HF Space webml-community/whisper-large-v3-turbo-webgpu](https://huggingface.co/spaces/webml-community/whisper-large-v3-turbo-webgpu); transformers.js v3 ships a WebGPU Whisper example — [Reddit/LocalLLaMA thread with source link](https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1daf8z1/webgpuaccelerated_realtime_inbrowser_speech/). An open issue reports fp16 encoder precision problems for Whisper on WebGPU — [transformers.js issue #1590](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers.js/issues/1590).
- whisper.cpp (MIT) is "optimized for CPU inference and Apple Silicon", repo at ggml-org/whisper.cpp — [Vexascribe methodology note](https://vexascribe.com/whisper-large-v3-vs-turbo); Whisper Notes ships large-v3-turbo on-device on iPhone/Mac "100+ languages, entirely offline" — [whispernotes.app](https://whispernotes.app/).
- Moonshine (Useful Sensors) specialised tiny ASR models cover English plus Arabic, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Ukrainian and Vietnamese — no Afrikaans — [arXiv 2509.02523](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02523v1).

### Inferences
- For a free Afrikaans service, the realistic "no server cost" path is a PWA running Whisper turbo (or small/medium for weaker phones) via WebGPU/WASM; cost moves to the user (1.6 GB download, a device with WebGPU and ~2–6 GB free memory). On-device OS APIs can be used only as a fallback for English (South Africa).
- Android's online `SpeechRecognizer` backed by Google's service very likely accepts af-ZA (Google's cloud voice typing lists Afrikaans), but this is cloud processing by Google, not on-device, and is device-dependent. Flagged inference.

### Gaps
- No authoritative list of Live Transcribe's 80+ languages was retrieved, so Afrikaans support in Live Transcribe is unconfirmed.
- No benchmark of in-browser Whisper turbo speed on typical South African mid-range Android phones.
- Apple's feature-availability page was only partially read (first 100k chars).

## Key Question 4: Cost model per hour of audio and monthly cost at 100 / 1,000 / 10,000 hours

### Takeaway
At 100 h/month a free Afrikaans service can run for $0 of compute (Cloudflare or Groq free allocations, or browser-side Whisper); at 1,000 h/month the cheapest managed APIs cost $12–$40/month; at 10,000 h/month per-minute Whisper APIs cost $120–$400/month versus roughly $250–$650/month for a dedicated 24/7 RTX 4090 that would be mostly idle.

## Key Question 5: POPIA obligations (voice recordings, information officer, cross-border, retention, exemptions)

### Takeaway
POPIA applies to any operator processing voice recordings in the course of a business or organised activity (the household exclusion protects only the individual user), the Information Regulator says even sole proprietors must register an Information Officer, voice data is arguably biometric "special personal information" under POPIA's definition, and sending audio to US-hosted APIs requires a section 72 ground (adequate-protection agreement, consent or contractual necessity); fines go up to R10 million.

### Cited Findings
- Section 6(1)(a): the Act does not apply to processing "in the course of a purely personal or household activity"; (b) de-identified information that cannot be re-identified — [POPIA s6 (popia.co.za)](https://popia.co.za/section-6-exclusions/).
- Information Regulator FAQ: "All organisations, both public and private bodies, are obliged to register their information officer with the Information Regulator... Individuals (Sole Proprietor), small businesses, and firms are also obliged to register their Information Officers if they process personal information"; registration is via the eServices portal and the IO may only take up duties after registration (s55(2)) — [Information Regulator POPIA page](https://inforegulator.org.za/popia/). "The exemption for small businesses ended on 31 December 2021" — [ClearComply](https://www.clearcomply.co.za/blog/popia-information-officer-registration-south-africa) (flagged: vendor blog). Every registered organisation's IO is automatically its head (CEO/MD or equivalent) — [Information Regulator BizPortal](https://inforegulator.bizportal.gov.za/Registration/Default.aspx).
- Section 37 exemptions: responsible parties may apply to the Regulator for exemption from conditions; the Regulator published guidance on exemption applications and IO registration — [Inside Privacy (Covington)](https://www.insideprivacy.com/uncategorized/south-africa-guidance-on-popia-exemptions-and-registration-of-information-officers/), [Webber Wentzel](https://www.webberwentzel.com/News/Pages/clarity-on-popia-exemptions-and-information-officer-registration.aspx).
- POPIA's definition of "biometrics" expressly includes "voice recognition" (alongside fingerprinting, DNA analysis, retinal scanning), and biometric information is special personal information — [POPIA Act text (gov.za PDF)](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/3706726-11act4of2013protectionofpersonalinforcorrect.pdf), [POPIA s1 definitions](https://popia.co.za/section-1-definitions/); commentary: "POPIA's definition of biometrics expressly includes voice recognition. POPIA also treats biometric information as part of 'special personal information'" — [Engelsman Attorneys](https://engelsman.co.za/buying-ai-software-popia-turns-your-tool-into-a-legal-relationship/); "Every call your contact centre records is personal information, and some of what you do with it is biometric processing" — [ASi Imperium](https://www.asitechnologies.co.za/contact-centre-popia-compliance).
- Section 72(1): a responsible party may transfer personal information to a third party in a foreign country only if (a) the recipient is subject to law, binding corporate rules or a binding agreement providing adequate protection with substantially similar onward-transfer rules, (b) the data subject consents, (c) the transfer is necessary for a contract with the data subject, (d) necessary for a contract in the data subject's interest, or (e) for the data subject's benefit where consent is not practicable but likely — [POPIA s72 (popia.co.za)](https://popia.co.za/section-72-transfers-of-personal-information-outside-republic/), [Dentons explainer](https://www.dentons.com/en/insights/articles/2022/october/13/cross-border-data-transfers-under-the-protection-of-personal-information-act-4-of-2013). Academic analysis notes POPIA, unlike GDPR, has no adequacy-decision mechanism and the operator (processor) transfer question is contested — [Coetzee, PER 2024](https://www.saflii.org/za/journals/PER/2024/48.html).
- Penalties: administrative fines capped at R10 million (s109(2)(c)); criminal penalties up to 10 years for e.g. failing to comply with an enforcement notice; in practice every fine to date followed an ignored enforcement notice — [MJ Kotze Inc](https://mjkinc.co.za/popia/fines-and-penalties), [ITLawCo 2026 briefing](https://itlawco.com/information-regulator-briefing-2026/). South Africa's first administrative fines were R5 million each — [Facebook post summarising the two R5m fines](https://www.facebook.com/BreakTheSilenceAboutSouthAfrica/posts/special-report-two-r5-million-popia-fines-but-two-very-different-privacy-battles/1488577259962615/) (flagged: social-media source; the R5m figure is also repeated at [Instagram](https://www.instagram.com/p/Dcf6gRtERU9/)).
- POPIA's eight conditions for lawful processing apply unless exempted under s37/s38 — [Information Regulator](https://inforegulator.org.za/popia/).

### Inferences
- A free service run by an individual as a public offering is still a "responsible party" (processing is not "purely personal or household"), so the hobby status does not exempt it; the practical minimum is: register an IO, publish a privacy notice/PAIA manual, process under a lawful basis (consent or legitimate interest), delete audio quickly (retention/minimality), and have a s72 ground for any foreign API (user consent plus a DPA with the provider).
- Whether plain transcription counts as "biometric" processing is debatable (transcription does not identify the speaker by voiceprint), but the conservative reading used by SA practitioners is that voice recordings can be special personal information, which would require s27 consent or another s27 justification. Flagged legal interpretation.
- Keeping processing in-country (or on-device/in-browser) removes the s72 problem entirely and is the simplest compliance posture for a free tool.

### Gaps
- No statement from the Information Regulator specifically about hobby/free or very small online services beyond the general "sole proprietors must register" FAQ.
- Michalsons' POPIA/voice-recording commentary could not be fetched (page body not returned).

## Key Question 6: Recording consent (RICA) and implications for a meeting transcription tool

### Takeaway
South Africa is effectively single-party consent: under RICA s4 a participant may record a conversation they are party to without the other parties' consent, while recording a conversation one is not party to requires an interception warrant — so a meeting-transcription tool is lawful for participants but must not be used to capture others' conversations, and POPIA still governs what happens to the recording afterwards.

### Cited Findings
- "Single-party consent – If you are a participant in the conversation, your consent alone is sufficient... Third-party interception – Recording a conversation between others, in which you are not a participant, is strictly prohibited without judicial authorisation" under RICA (Act 70 of 2002), s2 interception warrant — [MFP Attorneys](https://www.mfp.co.za/employment-and-labour-law/recording-conversations-in-south-africa-what-you-need-to-know/).
- "According to Section 4 of RICA, it is generally permissible for an individual to record or intercept a conversation if they are a party to the conversation" — [LabourMan Consultants](https://labourman.co.za/recording-conversations-without-consent/).
- Contradiction: Michalsons' article snippet states "Recording without consent is illegal. No one may record a conversation without the consent of all parties involved in that conversation" — [Michalsons](https://www.michalsons.com/blog/recording-conversations-without-consent/923) (flagged: the full article could not be fetched; the snippet likely addresses non-party recording, since the two law-firm sources above and the Act's s4 party exception say otherwise).
- Consent is central: Section 4 (party to the communication) and Section 5 (written consent of a party) are the standard lawful bases — [MFP Attorneys](https://www.mfp.co.za/employment-and-labour-law/recording-conversations-in-south-africa-what-you-need-to-know/).

### Inferences
- For a meeting-bot product, the user who invites the bot must be a participant; best practice (and POPIA transparency duties) favour announcing the recording to other attendees even though RICA does not require their consent.

### Gaps
- No case law or Regulator guidance specifically on AI meeting bots in South Africa was found.

### Inferences
- Addressable base: roughly 6–7 million L1 Afrikaans speakers (SA + Namibia) and up to ~10 million L2 speakers, with internet and WhatsApp penetration high enough that most are reachable via a WhatsApp-first product.
- Because Afrikaans is declining as a home language and concentrated regionally (Western/Northern Cape), marketing can be geographically targeted.

## Key Question 11: How do "free" transcription tools fund themselves and what are typical free-tier limits?

### Takeaway
Free transcription products are almost all freemium with hard caps (Otter 300 min/month, TurboScribe 3 files/day of 30 min, HappyScribe 10 trial minutes, Sonix 30 trial minutes, Vulavula 100 API calls/month), or they are open-source software the user self-hosts; provider free allocations (Cloudflare 10k neurons/day, Groq daily audio caps, Deepgram $200 credit, HF ZeroGPU) are the main way a genuinely free hosted service can stay at $0.

### Cited Findings
- Otter.ai Basic: 300 min/month, 30 min per conversation, 3 lifetime imports, 25 most recent conversations, 20 AI chat questions/month; paid Pro $8.33/user/month annual — [Scribbl, as of 7 Oct 2026](https://scribbl.co/post/otter-ai-pricing), [Otter](https://otter.ai/start-for-free).
- TurboScribe Free: 3 transcripts/day, 30-minute uploads, "Lower Priority"; Unlimited $10/month billed yearly — [TurboScribe](https://turboscribe.ai/free-transcription/Afrikaans); "No training data will be made from the files you upload" — [meetjamie](https://www.meetjamie.ai/blog/free-transcription-software).
- HappyScribe: 10 free AI minutes; free plan cannot top up; unlimited 45-minute meeting recordings on the free plan — [HappyScribe pricing](https://www.happyscribe.com/pricing), [meetjamie](https://www.meetjamie.ai/blog/free-transcription-software).
- Sonix: 30 minutes free, then $10/hr PAYG — [Sonix pricing](https://sonix.ai/pricing).
- Vulavula: Free 100 API calls/month, Dev Pass $9.99 — [Lelapa plans](https://docs.lelapa.ai/overview/plans).
- Transkripsie: one-time 60 free upload minutes, then $24/month — [Transkripsie](https://www.transkripsie.com/pricing).
- Open-source/self-host: OpenWhispr ("Open source and on device"), whisper.cpp (MIT), Whisper Notes (offline iPhone/Mac app) — [OpenWhispr](https://openwhispr.com/), [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/open-source-voice-dictation-tools-wispr-alternatives), [Whisper Notes](https://whispernotes.app/); AssemblyAI's own guide concedes "the best free option is an open-source engine, and OpenAI's Whisper is the most capable" — [AssemblyAI blog](https://www.assemblyai.com/blog/the-top-free-speech-to-text-apis-and-open-source-engines).
- Provider free allocations: Cloudflare 10,000 neurons/day — [Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/); Groq Whisper 28,800 audio-s/day base limits — [Groq](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits); Deepgram $200 credit — [Deepgram](https://deepgram.com/pricing); HF ZeroGPU free Spaces — [HF pricing](https://huggingface.co/pricing); Gemini free tier exists — [Gemini rate limits](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/rate-limits).

### Inferences
- Sustainable "free" designs, in order of cost: (1) browser/on-device Whisper (compute cost zero; funding optional via donations or a paid cloud tier for long files/meeting bots); (2) Cloudflare Workers AI within the daily free allocation (~107 h/month) with a daily per-user cap; (3) a Deepinfra/Groq-backed free tier capped at e.g. 60 min/user/month, which costs ~$0.012–$0.04 per user-hour and can be cross-subsidised by a $5–10/month unlimited plan (TurboScribe's model) or by B2B (church, legal, call-centre) plans.
- Typical free-tier caps in the market (10–300 min/month or 3 files/day) are the reference point users will compare against.

### Gaps
- No sourced examples of donation- or sponsorship-funded transcription services, nor ad-supported ones, were found in this pass; "donations" and "sponsorship" remain untested models for this niche.

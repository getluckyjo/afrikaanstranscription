# Wat sê jy: Carla's business

_One page for Carla, October 2026. Johannes builds and maintains the tool; Carla owns and runs Wat sê jy and keeps all of the profit. Figures are planning estimates at R17.50 per US dollar, not promises. Legal setup is costed at zero because it is being arranged for free._

## The idea in one paragraph

Afrikaans speakers send voice notes and sit in meetings all day, and nothing turns that speech into good Afrikaans text. WhatsApp's own transcripts skip Afrikaans. The big tools get a third to two thirds of Afrikaans words wrong. Wat sê jy runs on the customer's own computer, uses a model tuned for Afrikaans, and gives back a transcript with speakers labelled that can be corrected line by line. Nothing leaves the machine, which is exactly what lawyers, doctors, churches and schools need. The working version already exists and runs on a Mac today.

## What Carla runs

**The data, which is the moat.** The models are public; anyone can download them. What nobody has is a few hours of real, consented, carefully corrected Afrikaans as people actually speak it. Five minutes of that teaches a model as much as fifty hours of the wrong kind. Building that collection is the first job and the most valuable thing in the business.

**The customers.** Finding the first churches, practices and schools, onboarding them, answering questions, and collecting payment.

**The product's voice.** Afrikaans wording, the help text, the website, the social accounts.

Johannes handles the code, the model, the app releases and the technical setup.

| When | What Carla does | Time |

| Month 1 | Record 2–5 hours of real meetings and voice notes, get consent forms signed, correct the drafts in the editor | 60–100 hours |

| Months 4–6 | First paying customers; 20–50 hours of training audio done; website and pricing live | 10–15 hours a week |

After launch, running costs are R5,000 to R15,000 a month at small scale. Transcribing an hour of audio on the customer's machine costs nothing, so margins on every paid plan are above 90%.

Beyond the profit, the corrected dataset and the tuned model are an asset in their own right: this kind of Afrikaans speech data exists nowhere else, and a buyer would pay for it even if the app never grew.

## What could go wrong

Meta could add Afrikaans to WhatsApp's own transcripts and take away the voice-note hook, though not the meeting and professional business where most of the money sits. South Africans are slow to pay for software, so churches and practices carry the plan, not individuals. Free models keep improving, which is why the corrected data and the customer relationships, not the engine, keep the lead.

## Four checkpoints

1. **Month 2:** the model gets under 20% of words wrong on our own recordings.
2. **Month 4:** fifty people using it weekly across two or three groups.
3. **Month 8:** ten paying churches or practices. Register the company in Carla's name at this point.
4. **Month 12:** base case in sight, or keep it as a free tool and a data asset.

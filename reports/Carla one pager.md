# Wat sê jy: the idea, and what Carla can earn

_One page for Carla, October 2026. Figures are planning estimates at R17.50 per US dollar, not promises. Legal setup is costed at zero because it is being arranged for free._

## The idea in one paragraph

Afrikaans speakers send voice notes and sit in meetings all day, and nothing turns that speech into good Afrikaans text. WhatsApp's own transcripts skip Afrikaans. The big tools get a third to two thirds of Afrikaans words wrong. Wat sê jy runs on the user's own computer, uses a model tuned for Afrikaans, and gives back a transcript with speakers labelled that you can correct line by line. Nothing leaves the machine, which is exactly what lawyers, doctors, churches and schools need. The working version already exists and runs on Johannes's Mac.

## Why Carla's part is the whole game

The transcription models are public and anyone can download them. What nobody has is a few hours of **real, consented, carefully corrected Afrikaans** as people actually speak it: fast, mixed with English, over a phone mic, in a noisy room. Research shows five minutes of that kind of audio teaches a model as much as fifty hours of the wrong kind. Whoever builds that collection owns the only part of Wat sê jy that cannot be copied. That is the data lead's job, and it is the most valuable work in the project.

**What it involves**

- Recruit people and groups, record real meetings and voice notes, get a signed consent form from every voice.
- Correct machine drafts against the audio in the editor. Press Enter, next line plays. Two to three hours of work per hour of audio.
- Keep the tally: hours by source, speakers by age, region and variety, forms filed.

**How much**

| Phase | Target | Carla's time |
|---|---|---|
| First month | 2–5 hours of audio, the test set | 60–100 hours |
| Months 2–6 | 20–50 hours of audio, the training set | 5–10 hours a week |

## What it costs to launch (legal at zero)

| Item | Estimate |
|---|---|
| GPU time for benchmarking and one fine-tune | R2,000–R5,000 |
| Apple and Windows signing so the app installs cleanly | R5,000–R10,000 |
| Domain, hosting, storage | R3,000 a year |
| Marketing to the first communities | R10,000–R20,000 |
| **Total cash** | **about R20,000–R40,000** |

Running costs after launch are R5,000 to R15,000 a month at small scale, and an hour of audio costs nothing to transcribe on the user's machine, so margins are above 90% on anything paid.

## Where the money comes from

Churches at R250–R500 a month for sermons and council minutes. Legal and accounting practices at R500–R1,500 a seat because the audio never leaves their office. Medical practices in the Cape. Municipalities and schools. Individuals at R90–R180 a month for voice notes. Later, licensing the model and data to bigger players.

## What Carla can earn

Two ways to be paid, which can be combined. Johannes and Carla decide the split; the numbers below only show the shape.

**1. Paid for the data work.** Professional Afrikaans transcription in South Africa pays transcribers roughly R25–R70 per audio hour for typing from scratch. Correcting a good machine draft is faster and more skilled, so a fair internal rate is **R200–R300 per audio hour**, or about R100 an hour of her time. The first-month test set is worth R600–R1,500; the full training set R4,000–R15,000.

**2. A share of the profit.** Twelve months after launch, after running costs:

| | Conservative | Base | Upside |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual revenue | R140,000 | R790,000 | R3.1 million |
| Annual running cost | R60,000 | R180,000 | R720,000 |
| **Annual profit** | **≈ R80,000** | **≈ R610,000** | **≈ R2.4 million** |
| Carla at a 25% share | R20,000 | R150,000 | R600,000 |
| Carla at a 50% share | R40,000 | R305,000 | R1.2 million |

The conservative case is a side income. The base case is a real salary for part-time hours. The upside case is a business. On top of the profit, the corrected dataset and the tuned model are an asset in their own right: Afrikaans speech data of this kind does not exist anywhere else, and a buyer would pay for it even if the app never grew.

## What could go wrong

Meta could add Afrikaans to WhatsApp's own transcripts and take away the voice-note hook, though not the meeting and professional business. South Africans are slow to pay for software, so churches and practices carry the plan, not individuals. The free models keep improving, which is why the corrected data, not the engine, is what keeps the lead.

## Four checkpoints

1. **Month 2:** the model gets under 20% of words wrong on our own recordings.
2. **Month 4:** fifty people using it weekly across two or three groups.
3. **Month 8:** ten paying churches or practices. Register the company at this point.
4. **Month 12:** base case in sight, or keep it as a free tool and a data asset.

### Kort opsomming in Afrikaans

Wat sê jy maak Afrikaanse stemboodskappe en vergaderings teks, op jou eie rekenaar, met 'n model wat vir Afrikaans afgerig is. Die program werk reeds. Die enigste deel wat niemand kan naboots nie, is 'n versameling egte, gekorrigeerde Afrikaanse opnames met toestemming, en dit is Carla se werk: mense werf, opnames maak, vorms laat teken en die masjienkonsepte reël vir reël regmaak. Die begin kos sowat R20,000 tot R40,000 kontant (regskoste is gratis). Carla kan vir die datawerk betaal word (ongeveer R200 tot R300 per uur oudio) en/of 'n aandeel in die wins kry: in die basisgeval sowat R150,000 tot R305,000 per jaar, afhangende van die aandeel.

# Fine-tuned Whisper makes free Afrikaans transcription viable

A free Afrikaans transcription tool for WhatsApp voice notes and meeting recordings is feasible for a small South African team, but only if it self-hosts an Afrikaans-fine-tuned Whisper model rather than relying on the cheap stock-Whisper APIs or on commercial providers. Stock Whisper large-v3 scores roughly **33–42% word error rate (WER) on read Afrikaans and 66–78% on South African telephone and local-accent corpora**, while three MIT/Apache-licensed Afrikaans fine-tunes released in 2026 (Fluister-turbo, BuzzASR/afrikaans, Sunbird SunflowerASR-51) reach **8.6–16% WER on read speech**, and the only model explicitly trained for Afrikaans–English code-switching (Fluister) runs in about 1.5 GB of GPU memory ([BuzzASR paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.09554); [Voice of a Continent](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18436); [Fluister model card](https://huggingface.co/digiphyte/fluister-turbo-transformers)). Compute is no longer the constraint: batched Whisper-turbo on a rented RTX 4090 costs well under $0.01 per audio hour, so **100 hours a month costs a few dollars, 1,000 hours tens of dollars, and 10,000 hours $100–650**, dominated by whether you rent a dedicated GPU ([TheStage benchmark](https://huggingface.co/TheStageAI/Elastic-whisper-large-v3-turbo); [RunPod pricing](https://www.runpod.io/pricing)). The real cost and risk drivers lie elsewhere: a WhatsApp Cloud API bot now pays Meta roughly **$0.0095 per transcript reply beyond 1,000 free replies per number per month** since 1 October 2026, which at volume outstrips transcription compute by an order of magnitude, while an Android PWA share target costs nothing per use ([Meta pricing updates](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing/updates-to-pricing); [ChatMaxima SA rates](https://chatmaxima.com/whatsapp-api-pricing/south-africa/)). POPIA applies in full even to a free service run by an individual, voice recordings sit close to "special personal information", and sending audio to any foreign server needs a section 72 ground, so consent, short retention and a registered Information Officer are non-negotiable from day one ([Information Regulator](https://inforegulator.org.za/popia/); [POPIA s72](https://popia.co.za/section-72-transfers-of-personal-information-outside-republic/)). No established free, accurate, Afrikaans-first consumer tool exists today, and WhatsApp's own on-device transcripts do not list Afrikaans, which leaves a clear opening ([WhatsApp Help Center](https://faq.whatsapp.com/241617298315321); [Transkripsie pricing](https://www.transkripsie.com/pricing)). The recommended build is a share-sheet-first PWA plus Telegram bot, a serverless GPU running Fluister in faster-whisper int8, pyannote diarization for meetings, a 24–48 hour audio retention policy, and a WhatsApp bot added only once a freemium or B2B tier can absorb Meta's per-reply fees; the whole thing can launch for under $25 a month and scale to 10,000 audio hours a month for a few hundred dollars.

## Afrikaans fine-tunes cut WER from 33% to under 16%

The single most important finding is that Afrikaans is not an intrinsically hard language for speech recognition; it is a badly under-represented one. Whisper's 680,000-hour training set contains only **4.1 hours of Afrikaans**, which is why the original paper reports FLEURS WER of 36.7% for large-v2, 44.9% for medium and 61.1% for small ([Radford et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.04356)). Independent 2026 evaluations put zero-shot large-v3 at **33.0% WER** on a combined FLEURS and Common Voice 25 test set, and the picture collapses on real South African audio: **66% WER on the Lwazi telephone corpus and 78% on NCHLT** ([BuzzASR model card](https://huggingface.co/BuzzASR/afrikaans); [Voice of a Continent, Table F.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18436)). Stock Whisper also "produces Dutch spellings" for Afrikaans, because it leans on the thousands of Dutch and German hours it did see ([Fluister model card](https://huggingface.co/digiphyte/fluister-turbo-transformers); [arXiv 2501.06478](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.06478)). The telephone-quality, local-accent regime of Lwazi and NCHLT is precisely the WhatsApp voice-note regime, so a tool built on stock Whisper, however cheap, would ship with transcripts that are wrong two words in three.

Fine-tuning changes this dramatically, and the work has already been done and published under permissive licences. The academic ceiling shows how far Afrikaans can go: fine-tuning Whisper on 50 hours of NCHLT read speech reaches **2.11% WER**, against 26.38% with only 1 hour ([Nahabwe et al., arXiv 2512.10968](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.10968)). Three ready-to-use models sit between those extremes. The table summarises the candidates; the WER figures come from different test sets and are not directly comparable.

Two further open families deserve a note mainly to rule them out. Meta's MMS-1B-all supports Afrikaans but is **CC-BY-NC 4.0**, which a company-run service cannot safely use even if it is free to end users, and it scores 92–119% WER on Lwazi and NCHLT ([facebook/mms-1b-all](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-1b-all); [Voice of a Continent](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18436)). SeamlessM4T-v2 is the best zero-shot option on South African data at 28% WER on NCHLT, but fine-tuned Whisper beats it by a factor of three ([Voice of a Continent](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.18436)). NVIDIA's Parakeet and Canary models, Mistral Voxtral, Kyutai STT and Moonshine do not list Afrikaans at all ([nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3](https://huggingface.co/nvidia/parakeet-tdt-0.6b-v3); [Voxtral Transcribe 2](https://mistral.ai/news/voxtral-transcribe-2)). Among commercial APIs, Afrikaans is being quietly dropped from the newest tiers (AssemblyAI Universal-3.5 Pro, Gladia Solaria-3, Deepgram Flux), and the best-documented options are ElevenLabs Scribe v2, which places Afrikaans in its "Good" 10–20% WER band, and Gemini 3.5 Transcribe, which lists af-ZA and documents mid-session code-mixing but publishes no Afrikaans-specific accuracy ([AssemblyAI languages](https://www.assemblyai.com/docs/speech-to-text/pre-recorded-audio/supported-languages); [ElevenLabs STT docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/capabilities/speech-to-text); [Gemini 3.5 Transcribe](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models/gemini-3.5-transcribe)).

Code-switching is the honest ceiling on quality. On AfriSwitch, the only in-the-wild conversational Afrikaans–English benchmark, the best zero-shot systems score **26% WER** (ElevenLabs Scribe 26.04%, Intron's Sahara V2.5 26.45%), Omnilingual 7B scores 30.4% and Gemini 36.9%, and every system was given the matrix-language label, so unprompted performance is worse ([AfriSwitch, arXiv 2608.26434](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.26434)). Fluister is the only open model that explicitly merged Afrikaans/English mixing into its weights and advises forcing `language="af"` so that embedded English words are still emitted while Afrikaans orthography is preserved, whereas auto-detection flips whole segments to English or Dutch ([Fluister model card](https://huggingface.co/digiphyte/fluister-turbo-transformers)). No one has yet published Fluister or BuzzASR numbers on AfriSwitch, so the team must measure this itself. The realistic expectation for a voice-note tool without in-domain fine-tuning is 15–25% WER on clean notes and 25–36% on heavily code-switched ones; sub-10% is only demonstrated on read speech.

The lever that moves those numbers is in-domain data, not model choice. The Stellenbosch study found that **5 minutes of in-domain audio "nearly matches" 52 hours of out-of-domain adult speech**, taking Whisper medium from 88.1% to 47.4% WER on spontaneous Afrikaans child narratives, with LoRA adding a further 6 points ([arXiv 2501.06478](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.06478)). The standard Hugging Face recipe fine-tunes Whisper small on ~8 hours in 5–10 hours on a Colab-class GPU and roughly halves WER ([HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/fine-tune-whisper)). About 100 hours of CC-licensed Afrikaans audio already exists for a second-stage fine-tune: NCHLT (56 h read, CC BY 3.0), andreoosthuizen/afrikaans-30s (~50 h spontaneous sermons and conversations, CC-BY-4.0, though its card contradictorily says "research only" and needs clarification with the author), FLEURS (~10 h) and Lwazi telephone speech (CC BY 2.5 ZA), while Common Voice Afrikaans is a negligible 0.76 validated hours ([SADiLaR NCHLT](https://repo.sadilar.org/handle/20.500.12185/280); [afrikaans-30s](https://huggingface.co/datasets/andreoosthuizen/afrikaans-30s); [Mozilla CV 25](https://datacollective.mozillafoundation.org/datasets/cmn29hngy0188mm07kzspi4d4)). The highest-payoff task for the team is therefore transcribing 2–5 hours of real, consented voice notes and meetings and running a short LoRA pass on Fluister; one 16–24 GB GPU for a day or two is sufficient.

Meetings add diarization, which is largely language-independent. pyannote speaker-diarization-3.1 is MIT-licensed, runs on CPU by default and reports 18.8–24.4% diarization error rate on AMI and AliMeeting, which is the realistic expectation for recorded Afrikaans meetings too ([pyannote 3.1](https://huggingface.co/pyannote/speaker-diarization-3.1)). WhisperX wraps it but has no Afrikaans wav2vec2 alignment model, so word-level timestamps need either the Dutch aligner as a validated hack or segment-level alignment only ([whisperx/alignment.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/m-bain/whisperX/main/whisperx/alignment.py)). On the commercial side Google Chirp 3 explicitly excludes Afrikaans from diarization, while Gemini 3.5 Transcribe, AWS, Azure batch and ElevenLabs document it without a language restriction ([Google STT languages](https://docs.cloud.google.com/speech-to-text/docs/speech-to-text-supported-languages)).

## Share-sheet ingestion is free; a WhatsApp bot costs $0.0095 per reply

There are three ways to get a WhatsApp voice note into the tool, and they differ enormously in cost and risk. The cheapest and safest is to have the user share the file. On Android a voice note is long-press → Share, or sits on disk as `WhatsApp/Media/WhatsApp Voice Notes/<yyyyww>/PTT-YYYYMMDD-WA00NN.opus`; on iPhone it is long-press → Forward → share icon → Save to Files ([IONOS guide](https://ionos.com/digitalguide/online-marketing/social-media/how-to-save-audio-from-whatsapp); [AirDroid](https://airdroid.com/file-transfer/whatsapp-audio-to-mp3)). An installed PWA can register as an Android share target through the manifest's `share_target` with `method: "POST"`, `enctype: "multipart/form-data"` and `files[].accept`, supported in Chrome for Android since version 76, which turns the whole flow into two taps with no Meta involvement, no per-message fee and no terms-of-service exposure ([MDN share_target](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Progressive_web_apps/Manifest/Reference/share_target); [Chrome Web Share Target](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/web-apis/web-share-target)). Safari does not implement Web Share Target (the WebKit request has been open since bug 194593), so iOS users need Save to Files plus a standard file upload, or a native share extension later ([WebKit Bugzilla 194593](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=194593)). Given that WhatsApp reaches roughly 94–96% of South Africa's 51.7 million internet users and the market is Android-dominant, this path alone covers most of the audience ([DataReportal Digital 2026](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-south-africa); [Yazi/GWI aggregation](https://www.askyazi.com/articles/whatsapp-penetration-across-africa-statistics-by-country)).

The official WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API) bot is technically simple but commercially changed on 1 October 2026. Inbound webhooks arrive as `messages[].type = "audio"` with `mime_type "audio/ogg; codecs=opus"` and `voice: true`; the bot fetches a download URL by media ID that **expires after 5 minutes**, with webhook media IDs valid for 7 days and inbound media capped at 100 MB ([Meta media reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/cloud-api/reference/media); [Meta audio webhook](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/webhooks/reference/messages/audio/)). Inbound notes are free, but Meta "resumed charging, on a per-message basis, for messages that were previously free": every business phone number gets **1,000 free service messages per month**, and the 1,001st transcript reply is billed at the market's fixed rate, which third-party rate cards put at **$0.0095 for South Africa**, billed in USD plus 15% VAT ([Meta pricing updates](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing/updates-to-pricing); [ChatMaxima SA rates](https://chatmaxima.com/whatsapp-api-pricing/south-africa/)). A South African outlet estimated $0.0076, probably the pre-increase utility rate, so plan on $0.0076–0.0095 per reply ([EVE Zone](https://evezone.evetech.co.za/daily-drop/whatsapp-business-reply-charge-sa-guide)). At 10,000 replies a month that is roughly $70–95 before VAT, about R1,200–1,700 at an assumed R17.5/USD, and a long transcript split across several messages multiplies it. Meta now also requires a payment method on file or deliveries stop at the free tier, so a "free for users" bot still needs a billed Meta account. Eligible non-profits keep free service messages through 31 December 2027, which matters if the team structures the project as an NPO ([Meta pricing updates](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing/updates-to-pricing)).

Access hurdles are manageable for a small team. A Meta-provided test number with up to five verified recipients is available immediately; production needs a Business Portfolio, a registered number, display-name review (typically a day or two) and the payment method ([Meta get started](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/cloud-api/get-started); [360dialog display-name guidelines](https://docs.360dialog.com/partner/waba-management/waba-profile-and-compliance/whatsapp-display-name-guidelines)). Business verification, which "can take several weeks", only gates business-initiated messages (the 250 → 2,000 → 10,000 tiers); replies inside the 24-hour service window opened by a user's voice note do not count, so a reply-only transcription bot never hits that ceiling ([Meta messaging limits](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/messaging-limits); [Twilio self sign-up](https://www.twilio.com/docs/whatsapp/self-sign-up)). Go direct to the Cloud API rather than through a BSP: Twilio adds **$0.005 per message inbound and outbound**, doubling Meta's charge on a reply-heavy bot, and 360dialog charges from €49 per number per month ([Twilio WhatsApp pricing](https://www.twilio.com/en-us/messaging/channels/whatsapp/pricing); [360dialog pricing](https://www.360dialog.com/pricing)). Twilio's shared sandbox remains the fastest way to prototype inbound audio before any approvals ([Twilio sandbox](https://www.twilio.com/docs/whatsapp/sandbox)). One residual risk: Meta's "AI Providers" policy, effective 15 January 2026, restricts general-purpose AI assistants on the platform outside a few legally mandated markets and does not say whether a narrow transcription bot counts; the definition is broad and no guidance exists ([Meta AI Providers policy](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing/ai-providers/)). Meta's Business Platform terms also forbid using platform data "to create, develop, train, or improve any machine learning or artificial intelligence systems" except fine-tuning for your exclusive use, which the fine-tuning plan above satisfies but a shared or published model would not ([Meta Terms for WhatsApp Business Platform](https://www.facebook.com/legal/Meta-Terms-for-WhatsApp-Business-Platform)).

Unofficial libraries are not an option for a public product. Both whatsapp-web.js and Baileys state that WhatsApp does not permit bots or unofficial clients and that bans are possible, WhatsApp's terms prohibit "auto-messaging" and software that "function[s] substantially the same as our Services", and malicious Baileys forks on npm have exfiltrated data from authenticated accounts ([whatsapp-web.js](https://github.com/pedroslopez/whatsapp-web.js); [Baileys](https://github.com/WhiskeySockets/Baileys); [WhatsApp ToS](https://www.whatsapp.com/legal/terms-of-service); [Vulert advisory](https://vulert.com/vuln-db/malicious-code-in-diezyclutch-baileys--npm-)). Telegram, by contrast, delivers voice notes to bots as a `voice` object, lets bots download up to 20 MB and message users at no cost with no verification, and uses the same OGG/Opus format, so it is a free secondary channel and demo surface even though its South African reach is far smaller ([Telegram Bot API](https://core.telegram.org/bots/api); [Telegram Bot FAQ](https://core.telegram.org/bots/faq)).

Meeting audio is best handled as files, not bots, in the first year. Phone-recorded in-person meetings arrive as m4a or aac through the same share-target and upload path. Browser recording via MediaRecorder works on Chrome (WebM/Opus) and Safari iOS 14.5+, but Safari emits only MP4/AAC, Safari-recorded blobs have caused poor Whisper transcripts, and standalone-PWA recording on iOS can silently stop when the screen locks, so the tool should use chunked, resumable uploads and tell iPhone users to record in Voice Memos and share the file ([WebKit MediaRecorder blog](https://webkit.org/blog/11353/mediarecorder-api/); [WebKit bug 252465](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=252465); [OpenAI community thread](https://community.openai.com/t/whisper-problem-with-audio-mp4-blobs-from-safari/322252)). Zoom, Meet and Teams cloud recordings can be exported by the host (Zoom via the recording-completed webhook and `download_url`, Meet to the "Meet Recordings" Drive folder, Teams to OneDrive or SharePoint) and uploaded ([Google Meet help](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/9308681); [Microsoft support](https://support.office.com/en-gb/article/ready-to-play-7d7e5dc5-9ae4-4b94-8589-27496037e8fa)). Meeting bots are the only ingestion path with meaningful per-use cost and an approval gate: Recall.ai charges **$0.50 per recording hour** with no platform fee, Vexa $0.30 per bot-hour or $0 self-hosted under Apache-2.0, Meeting BaaS $0.35–0.50, and any Zoom bot that joins other accounts' meetings needs Zoom App Review (about 4–6 weeks) plus a raw-data entitlement ([Recall.ai pricing](https://recall.ai/blog/new-recall-ai-pricing-for-2026); [Vexa pricing](https://vexa.ai/pricing); [Meeting BaaS pricing](https://meetingbaas.com/pricing); [Zoom SDK review](https://developers.zoom.us/docs/distribute/sdk-feature-review-requirements/)). Whatever the source, one ffmpeg call normalises everything to 16 kHz mono PCM (`ffmpeg -i in -vn -ac 1 -ar 16000 -c:a pcm_s16le out.wav`), which is what Whisper does internally anyway; 16 kHz WAV is about 1.9 MB per minute, so hour-long meetings should be kept as FLAC and fed in roughly 10-minute chunks ([openai/whisper load_audio](https://www.mintlify.com/openai/whisper/api/load-audio)).

## Compute costs collapse to cents per audio hour

The economics of Whisper inference have shifted so far that transcription compute is a rounding error next to messaging fees and people. The cheapest managed APIs now run Whisper large-v3-turbo for **$0.012–0.04 per audio hour**: Deepinfra at $0.0002 per minute, Cloudflare Workers AI at $0.0005 per minute with 10,000 free neurons a day (about 214 audio minutes, or roughly 107 hours a month, at $0), and Groq at $0.04 per hour with base limits of 28,800 audio-seconds a day ([Deepinfra](https://deepinfra.com/openai/whisper-large-v3-turbo/api); [Cloudflare Workers AI pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/); [Groq rate limits](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits)). The catch is that every one of these free or near-free endpoints serves stock Whisper, the model that is unusable for Afrikaans. The quality path requires self-hosting a fine-tuned checkpoint, which means GPU time rather than per-minute APIs. Fortunately that is cheap too. A rented RTX 4090 costs **$0.13–0.89 per hour** depending on marketplace (Vast.ai floor $0.13, median $0.49; RunPod Community from $0.34, Secure $0.89) and turbo transcribes at **RTFx 164 unbatched and 779 at batch size 24** on that card, so a busy 4090 processes hundreds of audio hours per GPU-hour ([Vast.ai pricing](https://vast.ai/pricing); [RunPod 4090](https://www.runpod.io/gpu-models/rtx-4090); [TheStage benchmark](https://huggingface.co/TheStageAI/Elastic-whisper-large-v3-turbo)). Allowing for voice-activity detection, I/O and short clips, a planning figure of 100–300x real time gives $0.002–0.009 per audio hour on a dedicated card. Serverless GPUs billed per second (RunPod 4090 at $1.10/hr, Modal L4 at $0.80/hr) land at an effective $0.007–0.013 per audio hour before cold starts, which matter for a workload of many 10–60 second voice notes ([RunPod serverless pricing](https://docs.runpod.io/serverless/pricing); [Modal pricing](https://modal.com/pricing)). The fine-tuned turbo checkpoint in faster-whisper int8 needs only **1,545 MB of VRAM**, versus 4,521 MB for full large-v3 fp16, so any 6 GB consumer GPU or a cheap L4 is enough ([faster-whisper issue #1030](https://github.com/SYSTRAN/faster-whisper/issues/1030)).

Everything around the model fits in free tiers at the 100–1,000 hour scale. Supabase Free gives two projects, a 500 MB database, 1 GB storage and 50,000 monthly active users, though projects pause after a week of inactivity; Vercel Hobby or Cloudflare Pages hosts the PWA; Cloudflare R2 offers 10 GB-month free with no egress fees and $0.015 per GB-month beyond that ([Supabase pricing](https://supabase.com/pricing); [R2 pricing](https://developers.cloudflare.com/r2/pricing/)). Storage is negligible with short retention: even 10,000 hours a month of ~1 MB/minute Opus is about 600 GB transient, which at 24–48 hour retention is roughly $9 a month. Hugging Face ZeroGPU Spaces host GPU demos for free and are a sensible place to put a public Fluister demo during Phase 0 ([HF pricing](https://huggingface.co/pricing)). The only cost line that scales badly is the WhatsApp bot: at 10,000 audio hours a month of one-minute voice notes, replies would run to hundreds of thousands of messages, or several thousand dollars at $0.0095 each, which is why the architecture below treats the bot as a capped convenience rather than the primary channel.

A practical commercial fallback is worth keeping in the design. Gemini 3.5 Transcribe at about $0.003–0.004 per minute, with documented code-switching and up to eight-speaker diarization, is the best price-to-feature option if the self-hosted path stalls, and ElevenLabs Scribe v2 is the best-documented quality option; but the Gemini free tier explicitly uses prompts "to improve our products", so only the paid tier is compatible with a privacy promise, and both process audio abroad ([Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing); [Gemini 3.5 Transcribe](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models/gemini-3.5-transcribe)). The only providers that can keep Afrikaans audio inside South Africa are AWS Transcribe in af-south-1 (Cape Town, batch and streaming) and Azure Speech in South Africa North (real-time and batch, but no Whisper, no custom training), neither of which runs a fine-tuned model ([AWS endpoints](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/general/latest/gr/transcribe.html); [Azure regions](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/regions)). In-country GPU rental for self-hosting was not surveyed in this research and remains a gap to close before Phase 3.

## POPIA binds a free tool; RICA permits participant recording

The team should assume the full weight of POPIA from the first user. Section 6 excludes only processing "in the course of a purely personal or household activity", which protects the individual forwarding a voice note but not the operator of a public service, free or otherwise ([POPIA s6](https://popia.co.za/section-6-exclusions/)). The Information Regulator's FAQ states that "Individuals (Sole Proprietor), small businesses, and firms are also obliged to register their Information Officers if they process personal information", that registration is via the eServices portal, and that the officer may only act after registration; the small-business exemption ended on 31 December 2021 ([Information Regulator](https://inforegulator.org.za/popia/); [ClearComply](https://www.clearcomply.co.za/blog/popia-information-officer-registration-south-africa)). Voice recordings carry an additional sensitivity: POPIA's definition of "biometrics" expressly includes "voice recognition", and biometric information is "special personal information", so South African practitioners read voice data conservatively as requiring section 27 consent or another section 27 justification ([POPIA Act text](https://www.gov.za/sites/default/files/gcis_document/201409/3706726-11act4of2013protectionofpersonalinforcorrect.pdf); [Engelsman Attorneys](https://engelsman.co.za/buying-ai-software-popia-turns-your-tool-into-a-legal-relationship/)). Whether plain transcription, which does not identify a speaker by voiceprint, is "biometric processing" is debatable, but a free tool gains nothing by testing that argument; explicit consent at upload is cheap. Administrative fines reach **R10 million**, and the first two fines issued were R5 million each, although every fine to date followed an ignored enforcement notice ([MJ Kotze Inc](https://mjkinc.co.za/popia/fines-and-penalties); [ITLawCo 2026 briefing](https://itlawco.com/information-regulator-briefing-2026/)).

Cross-border transfer is the clause that shapes the architecture. Section 72 permits transferring personal information abroad only where the recipient is bound by law or agreement providing adequate protection, the data subject consents, or the transfer is necessary for a contract with the data subject; POPIA has no GDPR-style adequacy mechanism and the position of operators (processors) is contested in the literature ([POPIA s72](https://popia.co.za/section-72-transfers-of-personal-information-outside-republic/); [Coetzee, PER 2024](https://www.saflii.org/za/journals/PER/2024/48.html)). This applies to a self-hosted model on a RunPod, Modal or Hetzner GPU in the US or Europe exactly as it applies to a Gemini call, so the tool needs an in-product consent step naming where processing occurs, a data-processing agreement with the GPU host, retention measured in hours, and a privacy notice and PAIA manual. Keeping processing in-country or on-device removes the section 72 problem entirely and is the simplest posture, which is a second argument for the browser-side WebGPU option as a later feature for sensitive users. On the model-licence side, Whisper and its derivatives (faster-whisper, whisper.cpp) are MIT and both Fluister and BuzzASR inherit MIT, so a free public service run by a company is in the clear; MMS and SeamlessM4T must be excluded as CC-BY-NC, Gemma sits under Google's custom terms, and Vulavula's Afrikaans model is proprietary ([facebook/mms-1b](https://huggingface.co/facebook/mms-1b); [Gemma Terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/terms); [Lelapa model card](https://docs.lelapa.ai/transcribe/model/afrikaans)).

Recording law is less of a problem than most founders fear. Under RICA section 4, a participant may record a conversation they are party to without the other parties' consent, while recording a conversation one is not party to requires an interception warrant, which makes South Africa effectively a single-party-consent jurisdiction ([MFP Attorneys](https://www.mfp.co.za/employment-and-labour-law/recording-conversations-in-south-africa-what-you-need-to-know/); [LabourMan Consultants](https://labourman.co.za/recording-conversations-without-consent/)). A Michalsons snippet asserting that all-party consent is required could not be read in full and likely addresses non-party recording ([Michalsons](https://www.michalsons.com/blog/recording-conversations-without-consent/923)). For a meeting feature the rule is simple: the user who uploads or invites a bot must be a participant, and POPIA's transparency duties make announcing the recording to attendees best practice even though RICA does not demand it. No South African case law or Regulator guidance on AI meeting bots was found, so this remains an interpretation rather than settled practice.

## No free, accurate Afrikaans-first tool exists yet

The competitive field has plenty of Afrikaans checkboxes and no established free, accurate, Afrikaans-first consumer product. WhatsApp's own Voice Message Transcripts, launched 21 November 2024, run on-device from language packs; the help centre lists "only English, Portuguese, Spanish, and Russian", a September 2026 Android beta added German, French, Italian, Japanese and Arabic variants, and iOS depends on Apple's dictation languages, which include English (South Africa) but not Afrikaans ([WhatsApp Help Center](https://faq.whatsapp.com/241617298315321); [WABetaInfo](https://wabetainfo.com/whatsapp-is-rolling-out-new-languages-for-voice-transcriptions/); [Apple feature availability](https://www.apple.com/ios/feature-availability/)). Google's Pixel Recorder re-transcribe adds isiXhosa, Setswana and Venda but not Afrikaans, ML Kit's on-device recognition omits it, and Teams captions do not offer it; only Google Meet lists "Afrikaans (beta)" live captions ([Pixel Phone Help](https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/16267698?hl=en); [ML Kit docs](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/genai/speech-recognition/android); [Google Meet help](https://support.google.com/meet/answer/15077804?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop)). The platforms are adding major world languages at a pace of a few per year, so native Afrikaans support looks unlikely in the near term, though this is inferred from press and beta coverage rather than an official roadmap.

Two things stand out. First, the price anchor for paid Afrikaans AI transcription runs from $10 per hour (Sonix) to $0.20 per minute (HappyScribe), with TurboScribe's $10–20 per month unlimited plan as the floor, so a free tool competes on convenience (share-sheet, Afrikaans UI, local privacy, code-switching) rather than price. Second, the only South African entrant with a general-domain Afrikaans product, Transkripsie, is pre-launch with pricing in flux, and the SA API vendor's own card warns its Afrikaans model is unsuitable for general conversation. The addressable base is real: Census 2022 counts **6,365,488 home-language Afrikaans speakers (10.6%)**, concentrated in the Western Cape (41.2%) and Northern Cape (54.6%), plus roughly 10% of Namibian households, inside a country with 79.6% internet penetration ([Stats SA Census 2022](https://census.statssa.gov.za/assets/documents/2022/P03014_Census_2022_Statistical_Release.pdf); [NSA Namibia](https://microdata.nsanamibia.com/index.php/catalog/9/variable/V19); [DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-south-africa)). Direct evidence of sector demand is thin, limited to call-centre QA (Vulavula's target), church platforms publishing Afrikaans transcript help and human-transcription agencies advertising Afrikaans work at R8.50 per audio minute, so the team should validate demand in Phase 0 rather than assume it ([Facebook post, Durbanville](https://www.facebook.com/whatsonindurbanville/posts/afrikaanse-transkripsie-projek-r850minuutons-benodig-gesoute-afrikaanse-transkri/960683897626284/)).

On sustainability, every "free" transcription product found is either freemium with hard caps or open-source software users self-host; no donation- or sponsorship-funded transcription service surfaced in this research, so those models are untested in this niche ([Otter](https://otter.ai/start-for-free); [AssemblyAI blog](https://www.assemblyai.com/blog/the-top-free-speech-to-text-apis-and-open-source-engines)). The arithmetic favours freemium. A free tier of 60–300 minutes per user per month costs the operator $0.01–0.04 per user-hour on self-hosted or Deepinfra-class compute, which a $5–10 per month unlimited plan (TurboScribe's model) and B2B plans for churches, legal practices and call centres can cross-subsidise many times over; the free tier itself should sit near the market's reference points of 10–300 minutes a month or 3 files a day. Structuring the entity as a non-profit would additionally keep WhatsApp service messages free until the end of 2027, and keeping the model open-source invites community data contributions, which the Stellenbosch results show are worth more than any engineering.

## Recommended architecture and a four-phase build plan

The recommended architecture is deliberately boring: a share-sheet-first PWA and a Telegram bot feeding an ffmpeg-normalised queue; a serverless GPU worker running Fluister-turbo in faster-whisper int8 with `language="af"` forced; pyannote 3.1 on the same worker for meetings, chunked at roughly 10 minutes; Supabase for auth and transcripts; R2 for audio with a 24–48 hour time-to-live; and a WhatsApp Cloud API bot added later, direct to Meta with no BSP, capped per user so that Meta's per-reply fees stay inside the 1,000 free messages or a paid tier. Gemini 3.5 Transcribe (paid tier only) is the documented fallback if a file fails or the GPU queue backs up. Consent to foreign processing, retention and the Information Officer registration are built before the first public user, not after.

Three decisions in this plan deserve justification. Fluister over BuzzASR as the default: both are MIT, but Fluister is turbo-sized (0.8B, ~1.5 GB int8, 2–8x faster), was trained on spontaneous Afrikaans plus South African English with code-switching merged in, and ships faster-whisper and Core ML builds, whereas BuzzASR is a 1.55B monolingual model trained on read speech; Phase 0 should confirm this on the team's own test set, and the two can be swapped without changing the pipeline because all three candidates share Whisper's runtime tooling ([Fluister](https://huggingface.co/digiphyte/fluister-turbo-transformers); [BuzzASR](https://huggingface.co/BuzzASR/afrikaans)). Serverless over the Cloudflare and Groq free tiers: those allocations are large (107 hours and up to 240 hours a month) but only serve stock Whisper, so they buy quantity of the wrong quality; they remain useful only as an English or emergency fallback. Share-sheet before bot: the bot is the better experience, but with Meta charging per reply from October 2026 it converts every free transcription into a marginal cost, while the PWA path costs nothing per use and carries none of the platform-terms or AI-Provider risk ([Meta pricing updates](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing/updates-to-pricing)).

Several uncertainties should be tracked explicitly. No Afrikaans WER exists for Fluister, BuzzASR or any commercial model on real WhatsApp voice notes; the 8.6–16% figures are on read or semi-clean speech, and the team's Phase 0 test set will be the first such measurement. Meta's official post-October-2026 South African rate is published only in a CSV rate card that could not be retrieved, so the $0.0076–0.0095 range is from secondary sources. Groq's free-tier audio caps are visible only in the account console. The afrikaans-30s dataset card says both "commercial use allowed" and "research only". Whether a narrow transcription bot falls under Meta's AI Providers policy is unanswered. In-country GPU hosting costs were not researched. And the biometric status of plain transcription under POPIA is a conservative reading, not a ruling.

## Conclusion

The question this research answers is not whether Afrikaans speech recognition is good enough, but where the cost actually sits once it is. Three independent groups solved the accuracy problem in 2026 by fine-tuning Whisper on roughly 100 hours of Creative Commons Afrikaans audio, and inference has become so cheap that a single rented consumer GPU could transcribe every Afrikaans voice note in the country for a few hundred dollars a month. What remains expensive is everything that touches Meta's platform and everything that touches personal data: each WhatsApp reply now has a price, and each upload creates POPIA obligations that a hobby framing does not escape. A team that accepts those two facts and designs around them, with share-sheet ingestion as the default, a bot as a capped convenience, hours-long retention and consent built in, can run a genuinely free Afrikaans tool indefinitely on freemium and B2B revenue.

The strategic implication is that the moat is data, not models. Fluister, BuzzASR and Sunflower are public, and anyone can deploy them; the first team to collect a few hours of consented, transcribed Afrikaans voice notes and code-switched meetings, and to feed them back into a LoRA pass, will own the only model measured and tuned on the real workload, and the Stellenbosch and AfriSwitch results say that five minutes of the right data beats fifty hours of the wrong kind. Building the consent and donation flow in Phase 1, before the WhatsApp bot and before scale, is therefore not a compliance chore but the product's core compounding asset.
